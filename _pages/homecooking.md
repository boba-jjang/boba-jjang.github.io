## Chef Choi Kang Rok's Ichiban / Niban Dashi

**Posted:** 2026-02-03

<div class="row pswp-gallery mb-3">
  <div class="col-sm-6 col-12 p-2">
    <a href="https://pub-fd13b860c4de4a389cda47ae184c8a40.r2.dev/posts/202602/2026_02_03_chef_choi_kang_roks_ichiban_niban_dashi.jpg" data-pswp-width="2160" data-pswp-height="1416" target="_blank" class="d-block rounded z-depth-1 hoverable overflow-hidden">
      <img src="https://pub-fd13b860c4de4a389cda47ae184c8a40.r2.dev/posts/202602/2026_02_03_chef_choi_kang_roks_ichiban_niban_dashi.jpg" class="img-fluid w-100" style="aspect-ratio: 3/2; object-fit: cover; background: #e8e2d9;" alt="Ichiban / Niban Dashi - photo">
    </a>
    <p class="text-muted small mt-1 mb-0">Photo</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Ichiban Dashi Ingredients

- Water 2L
- Dried kelp 40g
- Katsuobushi 60g

### Niban Dashi Ingredients

- Water 2L
- Leftover Ichiban Dashi ingredients
- Katsuobushi 30g

### Important Notes

- Heat the water with dried kelp to ~80°C and hold for 10 minutes (typically ~60°C if we have time, like 1 to 2 hours).
- Do not boil — higher temperatures will cause bitterness.
- Remove the kelp after 10 minutes.
- When the broth reaches ~90°C, add the katsuobushi (hot, but not boiling).
- Turn off heat and extract gently.

### Niban Dashi

- Add leftover Ichiban Dashi ingredients to fresh water.
- Boil until ~10% of the water has reduced.
- Add 30 g katsuobushi, then immediately extract.
- Do not overcook after adding katsuobushi.
